Privacy

Skywyrm is a free dragon game, made for kids and their families. Here is everything it knows about you, which is almost nothing.

  • No accounts, no passwords, no chat.
  • No ads and nothing to buy.
  • No cookies and no tracking or analytics companies.
  • We count a few game and visit numbers that can't tell players apart, so we can make the game better.

What we count

When a dragon's life ends in a solo game, your browser sends our game server one short note with just these numbers:

  • which world you flew in (Skyhold or The Orb)
  • how long your dragon grew, in metres
  • how long you flew, in seconds
  • how many dragons you beat
  • how it ended: the edge of the world, a tail, a head-on bump or leaving to the menu

When you play online, the game server counts the same numbers itself when a life ends in a room (or you leave), plus how many players were online at once and how many servers were made each day.

And once per visit, when you close the game or switch away from it, your browser sends one more short note:

  • where the visit came from, as one word: straight to the game (typed in or a bookmark), a search engine, a social site, a video site, a games site, or somewhere else. Your browser works this out by itself: the address of the page you came from is never sent
  • a short tag, if the link you opened had one of ours in it (like "qr" for a printed code or "invite" for a friend's invite). Any other tag is ignored, and the game takes it out of the address bar so it isn't passed on
  • roughly how long ago this device first visited: new, today, yesterday, 2 to 6 days, a week, 8 to 29 days, or a month or more. The dates of your first and last visit stay in this browser; only that rough answer is sent, and only with the first visit of the day
  • whether this is the device's first visit today
  • whether it has a touch screen or a mouse
  • how far you got: opened the game, started playing, finished a life, played again, or played online
  • how many lives you finished and how many seconds you played
  • whether you sent a friend an invite link

None of these notes has a name, an ID or a cookie, so nobody can tell who sent one, or that two notes came from the same person. The server doesn't keep the notes: it adds each answer to its own count for the day (one more visit from a search engine, one more visit on a touch screen, and so on), so the answers from one visit can't be put back together. It keeps the daily totals for 30 days and then deletes them. Anyone can look at the totals: here they are (they look like computer code, which is normal).

We use them to make the game more fun, for example to see whether new dragons crash too soon, a world is too hard, or new players give up before they start.

What stays on your device

Your dragon's name, rank, jade, perks, colours and settings, and the dates of your first and last visit, are saved in this browser on your device and are never sent to us. Clearing this site's data in your browser wipes them.

The one exception is playing online: the other players in that room see your dragon's name, after it passes our name filter (no rude words, links or email addresses). The server forgets it when you leave. If you create a server, its name shows in the server list until 12 hours after the last player leaves.

If you invite a friend to a server, the link holds only that server's code and the word "invite", never your dragon's name. Your friend sees that server at the top of their list and decides whether to join.

Internet addresses

Every website your device visits can see its internet address (its IP address). That's simply how the internet works. Our game server uses it for a moment to stop spam, for example to limit how many servers one home or school can create in an hour. It keeps it only in memory, for an hour at most, and never saves it or adds it to the totals.

The companies that host Skywyrm keep their own standard security logs, which include IP addresses: Google Cloud keeps its request logs for 30 days, and GitHub logs visits to the website too.

Who runs what

  • The game itself, this website, is hosted by GitHub Pages.
  • The online servers run on Google Cloud.
  • Everything the game needs (its code, fonts and sounds) comes from this website, so playing doesn't send anything to any other company.

For grown-ups

The totals can't tell players apart, so there is nothing in them to look up or delete for one person.

Grown-ups: questions or requests → https://github.com/joelisjoel/skywyrm/issues
